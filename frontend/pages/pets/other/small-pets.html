Meet the Companions

Popular Small Pet Species

Understand the unique social structures, behavioral patterns, and space needs of each small mammal.

Solitary • Nocturnal

Syrian Hamster

Mesocricetus auratus

Syrian hamsters are affectionate, solitary pets that must live strictly alone once mature. They are primarily nocturnal and need deep bedding (6–10 inches) for instinctive burrowing.

Lifespan2 – 3 Years
Min Enclosure450+ sq in
Social NeedStrictly Solitary
Wheel Size11 – 12 in (Solid)
Social in Pairs • Diurnal

Guinea Pig (Cavy)

Cavia porcellus

Vocal and social herd animals that thrive in bonded pairs or small groups. Unlike most pets, guinea pigs cannot synthesize Vitamin C and require fresh daily bell peppers or stabilized pellets.

Lifespan5 – 8 Years
Min Enclosure8 – 10 sq ft
Social NeedPairs / Groups
Dietary KeyDaily Vitamin C
Tiny & Fast • Crepuscular

Roborovski & Dwarf Hamster

Phodopus sungorus / roborovskii

Extremely speedy and delightful to observe. Roborovski and Winter White hamsters love wide sand baths for grooming their coats and multiple hideouts for feeling secure.

Lifespan1.5 – 3 Years
Min Enclosure450+ sq in
Key FeatureSand Bath Area
Wheel Size8 – 10 in (Solid)
Burrower • Social Pair

Mongolian Gerbil

Meriones unguiculatus

Desert diggers with boundless energy. Gerbils produce very little odor and love chewing plain cardboard rolls. They should be kept in bonded same-sex pairs in high-sided glass terrariums.

Lifespan3 – 4 Years
Housing20+ Gallon Tank
Bedding Depth8 – 12 Inches
Social NeedPairs Only
Velvety • Cool Climate

Chinchilla

Chinchilla lanigera

Famous for having the densest fur in the animal kingdom. Chinchillas are prone to heatstroke and must be kept in rooms strictly under 70°F (21°C). They clean themselves exclusively in volcanic dust baths.

Lifespan10 – 15+ Years
Temp Range60°F – 68°F Max
GroomingVolcanic Dust Bath
Cage StyleMulti-Level Tall
High Intelligence • Affectionate

Fancy Rat

Rattus norvegicus domestica

Often described as pocket puppies, fancy rats are clean, empathetic, and easily trained to do tricks. They suffer deep depression if housed alone, making bonded pairs or trios an absolute necessity.

Lifespan2 – 3 Years
Social NeedMust Have Companions
HousingTall Wire Cage (½″ gap)
EnrichmentHammocks & Puzzles
Habitat Mastery

Creating an Enriching Sanctuary

A spacious, well-ventilated enclosure is the foundation of lifelong health for small animals.

📦

Spacious Enclosures

Commercial pet store cages are almost always too small. Ensure floor space meets modern ethical minimums:

  • Hamsters: Minimum 450 – 800 sq inches unbroken floor space.
  • Guinea Pigs: Minimum 7.5 to 10.5 square feet C&C grid cage.
  • Rats: Minimum 2.5 cubic feet per rat in a tall, climbing cage.
🌱

Safe Bedding Substrates

Respiratory health depends on choosing dust-free, non-toxic bedding:

  • Recommended: Unscented virgin paper pulp, Aspen shavings, or washed fleece with absorbent pads.
  • DANGEROUS: Cedar and untreated Pine shavings contain toxic aromatic phenols that damage small lungs and livers.
  • Fluffy cotton nesting material: Highly hazardous (causes limb entanglement and intestinal blockage).
🎡

Safe Exercise Wheels

Running is essential for small mammal cardiovascular health, but wheel design matters:

  • Must have a solid running surface without wire rungs or mesh that can trap tiny paws (bumblefoot risk).
  • Diameter must be large enough that the animal's back remains completely straight while running.
  • Hamsters need 8″ (dwarf) or 11″–12″ (Syrian) wheels.
Nutritional Balance

Feeding for Longevity & Vitality

Small herbivores and omnivores have delicate digestive systems that require exact fiber-to-protein balance.

🌾

Unlimited High-Fiber Hay

For Guinea Pigs and Chinchillas, 80% to 85% of their daily diet must consist of fresh Timothy, Orchard, or Meadow Hay.

  • Continuous chewing grinds down open-rooted teeth.
  • Fiber prevents life-threatening gastrointestinal stasis.
  • Provide hay in clean racks 24/7.
🥦

Fresh Veggies & Vitamin C

Guinea pigs cannot produce Vitamin C and require 10–30 mg daily from food:

  • Top Foods: Yellow/red bell peppers, dark leafy greens (romaine, kale, cilantro).
  • Avoid iceberg lettuce (high water, no nutritional value).
  • Offer sugary fruits (apple, strawberry) as rare weekly treats only.
💧

Fresh Water & Lab Pellets

Feed uniform extruded pellets rather than muesli seed mixes to prevent selective feeding:

  • Provide clean water daily via heavy ceramic bowls or ball-bearing water bottles.
  • Clean water bottles weekly to eliminate bacteria buildup.
  • Avoid muesli mixes containing dyed sugary corn or sunflower seeds.
Best Practices

Small Pet Care Do's & Don'ts

Keep these fundamental guidelines at hand to ensure your pocket pet thrives.

✔ Care Do's

  • DO provide deep bedding for hamsters and gerbils so they can satisfy their innate burrowing instincts.
  • DO adopt guinea pigs and rats in pairs; solitude causes severe distress in social species.
  • DO weigh your small pet weekly on a gram kitchen scale; weight loss is the earliest indicator of illness.
  • DO offer safe chew toys made of untreated apple wood, willow balls, or seagrass twists to keep incisors trimmed.
  • DO locate an exotic veterinarian who specializes in pocket pets before bringing your pet home.

✖ Care Don'ts

  • DON'T use exercise balls (plastic hamsters balls); they offer poor ventilation, disorient pets, and cause stress.
  • DON'T house Syrian hamsters together; they will fight territorially, often with fatal consequences.
  • DON'T use scented bedding or pine/cedar; aromatic oils irritate delicate rodent respiratory tracts.
  • DON'T bathe chinchillas in water; water ruins their dense coat and leads to fungal rot. Use dust baths only.
  • DON'T house guinea pigs with rabbits; rabbits can carry Bordetella bacteria and may injure guinea pigs with strong kicks.
